What this shows

Standard portfolio optimization with two assets (stocks and bonds) produces a frontier curve and a single tangency point — the allocation that maximizes the Sharpe ratio. This widget shows what happens when the parameters themselves are uncertain.

Set the central value and uncertainty for each parameter. The widget runs many Monte Carlo draws — each draw samples a complete set of parameters from your specified distributions, computes the resulting frontier and tangency point, and adds them to the plot. The cloud of faint curves shows how much the entire frontier shifts under parameter uncertainty; the cloud of dots shows the corresponding tangency points.

Use Step through draws to highlight individual draws one at a time — each step shows one frontier curve and its tangency point as a matched pair. Or click a histogram bar to see which frontier curves and tangency points correspond to a given allocation range.

What to notice

Even modest uncertainty about returns, volatilities, or correlation produces a visibly wide spread in “optimal” allocations. The same data, with mild uncertainty acknowledged, supports a range of best-allocation answers.

This is a simpler precursor to a three-asset version. With two assets, the optimization is trivial (there’s only one allocation per stock weight), but the parameter sensitivity is already visible. With three or more assets, the optimization becomes a real choice, and parameter sensitivity gets richer.
